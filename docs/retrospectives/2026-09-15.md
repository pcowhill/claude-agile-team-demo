## Went well

**One customer complaint became three measured fixes, each of which found
the next.** The customer's 2026-09-10 comment on #425 — 1–2 s per frame in
the Zoom editor's Show result mode, and "please add the loop capability" —
was, within eight minutes, a P1 bug (#458) and a rewritten, unblocked #425.
#462 fixed what #458 named (fresh elements on every stop, N concurrent
renders never dropped) and measured its fixture at 808 / 815 / 41 ms before,
14–18 ms after. *Reviewing* #462 found the second half: the presented-frame
wait losing a race on about half of seeks and resolving on a 300 ms
fallback timer — a trimodal 40 / 315 / 805 ms distribution, diagnosed by
instrumenting the callback — filed as #465 and fixed in #466 (14–62 ms
after). *Implementing* the loop (#467) found the third: seeks on the app's
own recordings decoding forward from a sparse keyframe, 264 ms median per
frame at 1080p, filed as #468 and fixed in #469. Three sessions, three PRs,
a before-and-after number on each, and none of the three issues existed
when the customer wrote the comment. The customer asked for one thing and
got the causes.

**Reviews measured rather than read, and produced something every time.**
Eight of the ten reviews re-ran the suites on the pushed head (the two that
did not were a one-line copy change, #456, and a rule-text PR, #452, each
checked against CI and by grep); every review of a UI-affecting PR — six
of six — re-took the screenshot and said what it showed; three re-ran the
mutation check, #454's in both directions. #451's review found the stale
Visual-editors hint and filed #453; #454's found the misplaced phrase and
filed #455 with a replacement, merging anyway because the error it replaced
was the worse one; #462's found #465; #469's found the file-size figure
below. Merge-and-file, with the reason for not blocking stated, was the
shape each time.

**Customer input turned into work the same evening.** #457 (feedback with
a screenshot, 20:55) became #460 (21:06) and was closed as captured
(21:07); #392's approval comment became #459; the #425 comment became
#458. One session then implemented all three (#462, #463, #464) within 20
minutes of one another — the WIP cap, filled with three genuinely
independent issues, each body naming the other two and the one README
convergence point.

**CI has been green since the incident.** Of the last 40 runs, the two
failures are 2026-09-09's Google apt-index incident (#447 / #448, last
retrospective). The 25 completed runs since (392–416) all passed on the
first attempt, and where a reviewer read the Playwright job it reported no
flaky retries (#466: 225 passed).

**The trigger fired at exactly ten.** Earlier periods overshot to 31; this
one held because the session counted again after merging. An observation,
not a proposal: the orientation step says count once, and a session that
merges implementation PRs changes the number. Counting again before
choosing the next action is cheap enough to do without a rule; it is
written down so the next session that merges two PRs thinks of it.

## Went poorly

**A criterion's parameters were substituted without a word, and the
number moved by two orders of magnitude.** #468's fourth criterion asked
for "the file-size change of a 30 s screen recording". #469's body stated
**+1.7%**, measured on a **3.5 s** canvas clip with a **box moving every
frame** — true of that clip, with a qualitative caveat in the risks
section that static screen recordings would see more. The claim comment
adjusted a *different* criterion explicitly, with the reason (the
screen-recording path has no fake source in headless Chromium, so a canvas
proxy), and did not mention that 30 s had become 3.5 s. The review ran the
measurement at the criterion's own parameters — 30 s, 1280×720, a static
panel of text with a small moving cursor — and read **+164%** (1,804,918 B
→ 4,757,694 B). Both numbers are true; only the second describes the
product, because recordings are what embedded project saves (#98) carry: a
10-minute 720p screen recording is ~95 MB in such a file instead of ~36 MB.

The merge decision was the same either way — one reversible constant, the
conventional interval, the customer's seek complaint genuinely eased — but
the trade-off shipped described as negligible when it is material, and only
a reviewer who re-ran the measurement instead of reading it found out. The
same session wrote the criterion, claimed the issue and opened the PR
inside nine minutes, so review was the only independent eye on the number.
The rules allow that and this is not an argument against it; it is an
argument for a criterion's parameters being part of the criterion. Nothing
in `development.md` or `product-management.md` says so today. → #471.

**Authorship concentrated again, and both catches came from the reviewer
re-measuring.** #462–#464 from one session (3 of 10), #467 and #469 from
another (2 of 10, the second from an issue that session had written from
its own measurement minutes earlier); six sessions for ten PRs. The
2026-09-06 retrospective's tripwire — a defect reaching `main` that a
second author would plausibly have caught — did not fire: #465 was caught
reviewing #462, the #469 figure was caught reviewing #469. Both were caught
by measurement, not by reading the diff. Recorded as context for #471 and
not as a proposal; the previous retrospective's evidence against a cap
below the WIP limit stands.

**Three PRs sat open for four days with the cap full.** #462–#464 opened
2026-09-10 21:25–21:45 and merged 2026-09-14 15:48–15:58, with no CI run in
between — no session ran. Nothing to change: sessions run when the
customer runs them, and the first to run cleared all three before doing
anything else, which is the priority order working. Recorded so nobody
reads the gap as neglect.

**A reviewer widened a test bound under the mechanical-fix carve-out, and
the next PR narrowed it back.** #462's reviewer found the new e2e's 500 ms
bound failing 3 of 10 locally, diagnosed the cause (#465), filed it,
widened the bound to 1500 ms in a test-only commit with the mechanism
named, and merged; #466 restored 500 ms once the cause was fixed. Handled
right, and it worked *because* the cause was diagnosed and filed first: a
widening without one would be "deleting coverage to get green" in slow
motion. One instance, handled well — an observation, not a proposal.
