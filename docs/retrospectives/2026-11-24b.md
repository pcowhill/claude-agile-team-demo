## Went well

**Review waited on nobody.** The median open-to-merge time was **52 min**,
against 131 last period. The longest was #900 at 158 min, then #889 at
100 min. Every other PR merged within 84 min. Last period's three longest PRs waited out quiet
hours with no session running. This period ran through the afternoon and
evening. After #900 merged at 22:02, the customer's `Go X` (#899) ran
sessions back to back. A PR opened by one subagent was reviewed by the next. #904's
co-author handoff crossed three sessions in 83 min.

**PRs got smaller.** One merge added more than 1000 lines: #889, at 1918.
Last period had six out of 12. #897 (926) and #904 (992) came close. The
rest were under 610.

**Both changes-required rounds ran by the book.**
- **#889.** The reviewer measured focus jumping to the first ▶ after every
  Add, and the list scrolling back to the top. It pushed the fix and
  declared itself a co-author. A third session showed the new regression
  test failing with the old dependencies, then merged.
- **#904.** The reviewer counted 119 files at 60 fps and 238 at 120 fps,
  where the PR promised 120 and 240. It fixed the tail on the branch. A
  third session checked off-grid ranges of its own (30, 34 and 90 files)
  and merged.

**A measured cause ended a watch before it started (#905).** `main`'s
Firefox job reported `1 flaky` on `export-size-limit.spec.ts`. The failing
attempt's own log explained it: a file at 102.6 % of its limit read "0.78
MB — over the 0.8 MB limit". So it was filed as an ordinary `bug`, not
`flake-watch`, as `quality-and-ci.md` says. #906 fixed both the sentence
and the spec 75 min after the issue was filed. The same flaky recurred
once, on #904's CI, in between. #904's review named it and its issue
rather than writing it off, which is #887's proposal in practice.

**Two export bugs found by measuring were fixed in the period.** #902 put
overlays at 0 s into the first frame over a slate (#898). #903 did the
same for a range that opens mid-overlay (#901), the case #902 named and
left to its own issue.

## Went poorly

**The top Ready issue was deferred four times (#862).** Each deferral was
recorded on the issue, as "Take the top issue first" asks:
- 16:37, after reviewing #889;
- 17:36, after #891;
- 18:16, after fixing `main` (#895) and reviewing #894;
- 21:15, after #902.

Every comment gave the same reason: the issue's footprint was too large for
the budget left. The third proposed splitting it under the issue. The
fourth measured the cost: 83 `withEffects` calls. A fifth session did step
1 (#907), and it merged in 36 min.

The rule did its job of making the deferral legible. Nothing in it moved
the work forward, though. The split that ended the deferrals was proposed
on the third pass and taken on the fifth. Proposed below.

**A loose assertion hid a sync defect since #269 (#908).** The MP3 export
opened on 0.1–0.15 s of silence. It found its way into the file because
the tap started recording when the mix graph was built, not when playback
began. `export-audio-mp3.spec.ts` checked `duration > 1.5` on a 2.6 s
sequence, a bound the defect could never cross. It was found only when
#866's WAV criterion ("within 50 ms") measured the same tap.

The same shape appears in both changes-required rounds above. Each
author's test exercised the one case where the defect cannot show:
- #889's e2e added only the first sound, which is where focus jumps to
  anyway;
- #904's tested 24 fps, where the tail was complete.

Review caught those two. Nothing caught #908 for months. Last period
recorded two guards that could not fail (#879, #884), and
`development.md`'s "shown failing once" rule found both. That rule covers
geometry assertions only. This is three more instances, outside geometry.
No change is proposed yet, because review caught two of the three. The
next retrospective should count again.
